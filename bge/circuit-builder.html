Circuit builder

LI: I am learning how current behaves in series and parallel circuits.

SC: I can build a complete circuit · I can control a lamp with a switch · I can measure current and voltage · I can describe how current splits in a parallel circuit.

10:00
Tasks

1.Build a circuit that makes a lamp light.

Hint

Current needs a complete loop: from one end of the cell, through the lamp, and back to the other end of the cell.

The lamp lights because there is a complete circuit: one unbroken loop from the cell, through the lamp and back.

2.Add a switch so that you can turn the lamp on and off. Tap the switch to test it.

Hint

The switch has to be in the same loop as the lamp. A new switch starts open.

An open switch makes a gap in the loop, so no current can flow anywhere in that loop.

3.Build a circuit with one cell and two lamps in series. Are the lamps brighter or dimmer than one lamp on its own?

Hint

Series means there is only one loop. The current goes through one lamp and then the other.

In series the lamps are dimmer than one lamp on its own: the cell has to push the current through both. Take one lamp out and the other goes off too.

4.Build a circuit with one cell and two lamps in parallel. How bright are they this time?

Hint

Parallel means each lamp has its own branch. Both ends of each lamp are joined to the cell.

In parallel each lamp is as bright as one lamp on its own, and if one is taken out the other stays on. That is why the lights in a house are wired in parallel.

5.Light a lamp, then use a voltmeter to measure the voltage across it.

Hint

Leave the lamp's circuit as it is. Join one end of the voltmeter to one end of the lamp, and the other end of the voltmeter to the other end of the lamp.

A voltmeter goes in parallel with the component, across it. With one lamp on one cell it reads 1.50 V.

6.Build two lamps in parallel with one cell. Use three ammeters to measure the current in each branch and the total current from the cell. What do you notice about the readings?

Hint

An ammeter goes in series: drop the ammeter onto a wire and it joins into the wire. Put one in each lamp's branch and one next to the cell.

The current splits at a junction and joins up again: the total current is the sum of the branch currents, 0.30 A + 0.30 A = 0.60 A. No current is used up.

Challenge7.Use one cell, two lamps and two switches. Build a circuit where each switch turns off one lamp and leaves the other lamp on.

Put the lamps in parallel with a switch in each branch. Opening a switch only breaks its own branch, so the other lamp stays on.

Short circuit! The current has a path with almost no resistance, so it is far too big. A real cell would get hot and go flat quickly.
